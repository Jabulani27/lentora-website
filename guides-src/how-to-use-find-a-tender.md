Find a Tender is now the central home for UK public sector opportunities. If you've never used it, the interface can feel bureaucratic — but the process is straightforward once you know the steps. Here's how to search it and register to bid.

## At a glance

- **Find a Tender (FTS)** is the central digital platform under the Procurement Act 2023, run by the Cabinet Office.
- You can **search without an account**; you only need to register when you want to bid.
- Registration is via **GOV.UK One Login**, after which you enter your organisation's details once.
- You reuse those details across bids with a **share code** — no re-keying the same information each time.
- Set up **saved searches and email alerts** so new opportunities come to you.

## Step 1: Search the notices

Go to **find-tender.service.gov.uk** and select "Search" to see all current notices. You can then narrow the list by:

- **Notice type** (e.g. tender notices, planned procurement notices — see below)
- **Suitability**, including a filter for "Small and medium-sized enterprises"
- **Contract location**, to focus on your region

Apply your filters and update the results. You don't need an account for any of this — searching is open to everyone.

## Step 2: Understand the notice types

The platform uses a set of standard notice types covering the whole procurement lifecycle. The ones suppliers watch most are:

- **Pipeline notice** — advance warning of large upcoming procurements
- **Preliminary market engagement notice** — the buyer is engaging the market before tendering
- **Planned procurement notice** — early notice of a specific upcoming tender
- **Tender notice** — the live opportunity you can bid on
- **Contract award notice** — who won, and the start of the standstill period
- **Contract details notice** — published after a contract is signed

Knowing the difference helps you act early. A pipeline or planned procurement notice is your cue to prepare; a tender notice is the one with a deadline. (See [contract notices, PINs and early market engagement](/guides/contract-notices-pins-early-market-engagement) for the full set.)

## Step 3: Register on the Central Digital Platform

When you find something to bid on, register your organisation. The platform breaks this into three steps:

1. **Register** via **GOV.UK One Login** and create your organisation account.
2. **Enter your details** — you'll need your **Companies House number** (or equivalent, such as a Charity Commission number) and registered address. The platform gathers core information including basic details, connected persons, qualifications, trade assurances, exclusions and financial information.
3. **Declare and share** — generate a **share code** that hands your verified information to a buyer's system.

The point of this design is "enter once, reuse many times." You don't re-type your company information for every bid — you provide a share code, and the buyer's portal pulls in your details.

## Step 4: Set up alerts

Rather than checking the site daily, **save your searches** and choose how often you want email alerts. That way, relevant notices land in your inbox as they're published.

Set the alerts wider than feels comfortable at first. A too-narrow saved search is the most common reason suppliers conclude there is "nothing out there" — buyers describe the same requirement in wildly different language, and a keyword that matches your own marketing copy will miss most of it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need an account to search Find a Tender?
No. Searching is open to everyone. You only need to register when you want to bid for a contract.

### How do I register on Find a Tender?
Register through GOV.UK One Login, then add your organisation's details (including your Companies House number and registered address). You then generate a share code to provide your information to buyers.

### What is a share code?
A share code lets you reuse your registered organisation information across multiple bids, instead of re-entering it each time. You give the code to the buyer's system, which pulls in your details.

### Can I get email alerts for new tenders?
Yes. Save a search on Find a Tender and set an alert frequency. Do the same on Contracts Finder and on the devolved portals that cover you — alerts are per-portal, not UK-wide.
