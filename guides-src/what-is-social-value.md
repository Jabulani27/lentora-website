"Social value" now appears in almost every public tender — and it can be the part that wins or loses you the contract. If you're not sure what buyers are actually asking for, this guide explains it.

## At a glance

- **Social value** is the wider economic, social and environmental benefit a contract can deliver — beyond the goods or service itself.
- The **Public Services (Social Value) Act 2012** requires authorities to consider this when buying services.
- Central government uses a **Social Value Model**, scored as part of the bid.
- Current guidance (**PPN 002**) sets a **minimum 10%** weighting for social value in central government contract awards.

## What does "social value" mean?

Social value is the extra benefit a contract creates for society and the economy, on top of what's being bought. For example, a cleaning or construction contract might also create local jobs, offer apprenticeships, support small businesses in the supply chain, or cut carbon emissions. Buyers increasingly want suppliers to deliver — and evidence — those wider benefits.

## The Public Services (Social Value) Act 2012

The **Public Services (Social Value) Act 2012** requires English and Welsh public authorities to consider, at the planning stage of a services procurement:

- how what they buy might **improve the economic, social and environmental wellbeing** of the area;
- how the procurement process itself might **secure that improvement**; and
- whether to **consult** on those points.

In short, it bakes "what wider good can this contract do?" into the early thinking of a procurement.

## The Social Value Model and the 10% rule

For central government, social value is assessed using a **Social Value Model**, which groups social value into themes and outcomes that bidders are scored against.

The current Procurement Policy Note, **PPN 002**, sets a **minimum weighting of 10%** of the total scores available for social value (or equivalent). Where buyers use an absolute methodology (such as price-per-quality-point), that 10% applies to the non-price/quality criteria.

There is essentially one permitted exception to the 10% minimum: where preliminary market engagement shows that applying it would significantly reduce competition because the market isn't yet mature enough to deliver social value in that area.

> Note: PPN 002 replaced the earlier social value guidance (PPN 06/20). Always check the version a specific tender refers to.

## How to win on social value

- **Answer what's asked.** Social value questions map to specific themes and outcomes — address those, not generic good intentions.
- **Be specific and measurable.** "We'll create 3 local apprenticeships in year one" beats "we support the community."
- **Make it relevant to the contract.** Buyers want social value tied to *this* contract and *this* area.
- **Evidence delivery.** Say how you'll track and report the commitments you make.

Social value is where small suppliers are often genuinely stronger than large ones, and where they most often under-sell themselves. Local employment, local supply chains and real community ties are worth marks — but only if you write them down as specific, measurable commitments rather than as a statement of good intentions.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is social value in simple terms?
The wider economic, social and environmental benefit a contract delivers beyond the product or service itself — such as local jobs, apprenticeships, supporting SMEs, or reducing carbon.

### Is social value a legal requirement?
The Public Services (Social Value) Act 2012 requires English and Welsh authorities to consider social value when procuring services. For central government, PPN 002 sets a minimum 10% weighting in the award.

### How much is social value worth in scoring?
PPN 002 sets a minimum of 10% of the available scores (or equivalent) for central government contracts.

### What replaced PPN 06/20?
PPN 002 is the current guidance on taking account of social value in central government contract awards.
