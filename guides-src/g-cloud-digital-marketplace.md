If you sell cloud hosting, software or support, **G-Cloud** is one of the most important routes to the UK public sector — and one of the simplest to understand once you know how it works.

## At a glance

- **G-Cloud** is a government framework and online catalogue for buying **cloud hosting, cloud software and cloud support** from pre-vetted suppliers.
- Suppliers list services on the **Digital Marketplace**; buyers search and **call off** without running a full tender.
- The current version is **G-Cloud 14** (agreement RM1557.14), running **29 October 2024 to 28 October 2026**.
- It has **three lots**: Cloud Hosting, Cloud Software and Cloud Support.
- It is run by the **Government Commercial Agency (GCA)** — the body that was **Crown Commercial Service (CCS)** until 1 April 2026.

## What is G-Cloud?

G-Cloud is the UK government's framework and catalogue for buying cloud-based technology. Suppliers are assessed and admitted to the framework, then list their services on the **Digital Marketplace**, where public sector buyers can find and buy them.

The big advantage for buyers is speed: because suppliers have already been vetted and standard terms agreed, a buyer can **call off** a service without going through a full, lengthy procurement. They define their need, search and shortlist services against consistent criteria, then make a direct award to the best-fit service using the framework's core terms.

## The three lots

G-Cloud 14 is organised into three lots:

- **Lot 1 — Cloud Hosting:** infrastructure and platform services (e.g. hosting, storage, compute).
- **Lot 2 — Cloud Software:** applications accessed over the internet (e.g. SaaS).
- **Lot 3 — Cloud Support:** services to help set up, migrate to, or run cloud solutions.

When you apply, you list each service under the relevant lot.

## Which version is live?

The current iteration is **G-Cloud 14 (RM1557.14)**, which started on **29 October 2024** and runs to **28 October 2026**. The frameworks refresh periodically — G-Cloud 14 will be replaced by **G-Cloud 15** in due course. Because a new iteration means a fresh chance to get on, it's worth watching for the next application window.

## Who runs it: CCS, now GCA

G-Cloud is managed by the **Government Commercial Agency (GCA)**. If you've worked with public procurement before, you'll know it as **Crown Commercial Service (CCS)** — CCS became the Government Commercial Agency on **1 April 2026**. (Our guide to [Crown Commercial Service & CCS frameworks](/guides/crown-commercial-service-ccs-frameworks) covers the wider set of agreements.)

## How to get on G-Cloud

1. **Wait for an application window.** You can only apply when a new iteration of the framework opens for applications.
2. **Prepare your service definitions and pricing.** Each service needs a clear description, a service definition document and transparent pricing.
3. **Apply and, if successful, publish your services** on the Digital Marketplace.
4. **Keep your listings sharp.** Buyers shortlist on what they can search and read — clear, specific, well-priced listings win.

G-Cloud is a catalogue buy rather than an advertised tender, so opportunities are not "won" in the usual sense — buyers browse and shortlist, and there is nothing public to respond to. That makes it a poor sole strategy: being on G-Cloud is not a pipeline. Treat it as one route among several, and keep watching the official portals for the digital and technology contracts that are advertised properly.
