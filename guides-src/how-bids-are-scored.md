Winning public work means understanding how buyers decide. Evaluation isn't a mystery — it follows published rules. This guide explains how bids are scored, what changed under the new Act, and what happens between "you've won" and the contract being signed.

## At a glance

- Bids are scored against **published award criteria** using a stated **assessment methodology**.
- The Procurement Act 2023 changed the award basis from **MEAT** to **MAT** — the **Most Advantageous Tender**.
- Price isn't everything: MAT reinforces that quality and other factors count, not just lowest cost.
- After scoring, buyers issue **assessment summaries**, then publish a **contract award notice**.
- That notice starts a **mandatory standstill period of eight working days** before the contract can be signed.

## Scoring against award criteria

Every competitive procurement publishes **award criteria** — the specific things your bid is judged on — and an **assessment methodology** setting out how those criteria are scored (for example, a scoring matrix with weightings). Evaluators score each bid against the criteria, apply the weightings, and rank the results.

The practical lesson for suppliers: read the criteria and weightings closely and answer them directly. A strong bid is one built around what's actually being scored (see [how to write a winning tender response](/guides/how-to-write-a-tender-response)).

## MEAT becomes MAT

Under the old rules, contracts were awarded to the "**Most Economically Advantageous Tender (MEAT)**." The Procurement Act 2023 renamed this the "**Most Advantageous Tender (MAT)**."

The change is a clarification rather than a reversal of policy. By dropping "economically," the Act reinforces a point that was always true but often forgotten: **buyers don't have to award on lowest price.** The Act defines the most advantageous tender as the one that satisfies the authority's requirements and **best satisfies the award criteria** — which routinely include quality, social value, and other non-price factors. So a well-priced, high-quality bid can and should beat a cheaper, weaker one.

## After scoring: assessment summaries

Once evaluation is complete, the buyer issues **assessment summaries** to suppliers. These explain how your bid was assessed — giving you the information to understand the decision and, if necessary, challenge it. In competitive procedures, assessment summaries must be issued **before** the contract award notice is published.

## The standstill period

Then comes a deliberate pause. The buyer publishes a **contract award notice**, which starts the **mandatory standstill period** — a **period of eight working days** beginning on the day the notice is published.

The standstill exists so unsuccessful bidders have a window to scrutinise the decision and raise a challenge before the contract is signed. The contract can't be entered into during this period. (Buyers can also choose a *voluntary* standstill, which must likewise be at least eight working days.)

So the sequence at the end of a procurement is:

1. Bids scored against the award criteria (MAT basis).
2. **Assessment summaries** issued to suppliers.
3. **Contract award notice** published — standstill clock starts.
4. **Eight working days** standstill.
5. Contract signed, then a **contract details notice** published.

## What this means for you

- **Win on value, not just price** — build quality and social value into your bid.
- **Read your assessment summary** if you don't win; it tells you where you lost points and how to improve next time.
- **Know the standstill** — it's the window to query a decision you think was wrong.

The single most useful habit: before writing anything, map the scoring matrix into a document skeleton, with the marks available written next to each section. It sounds mechanical, and it is — but it stops you spending 800 words on a question worth 5% and two lines on one worth 30%.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does MAT mean?
Most Advantageous Tender — the basis on which public contracts are awarded under the Procurement Act 2023. It replaced "Most Economically Advantageous Tender (MEAT)" and reinforces that price isn't the only factor.

### Are contracts awarded to the lowest price?
Not necessarily. The most advantageous tender is the one that best satisfies the published award criteria, which usually include quality and social value alongside price.

### What is an assessment summary?
A summary the buyer issues to each supplier explaining how their bid was assessed. In competitive procedures it must be issued before the contract award notice.

### How long is the standstill period?
The mandatory standstill is eight working days, beginning the day the contract award notice is published. The contract can't be signed during this period.
