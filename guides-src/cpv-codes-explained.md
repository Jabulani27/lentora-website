If you've browsed public tenders, you'll have seen strings of digits labelled "CPV." They look cryptic, but they're one of the most useful filtering tools you have — once you know how to read them.

## At a glance

- **CPV** stands for **Common Procurement Vocabulary** — a standard classification system for what's being bought.
- Each code describes a category of **goods, works or services**, so buyers and suppliers use a shared language.
- A CPV code is **eight digits plus a ninth check digit**, arranged from broad to specific.
- UK tenders still use CPV codes under the Procurement Act 2023, so they're a reliable way to filter opportunities.

## What is a CPV code?

The **Common Procurement Vocabulary** is a single classification system that standardises how contracting authorities describe the subject of a procurement. Instead of one buyer writing "IT support" and another "technology helpdesk," both attach the same CPV code — so a search on that code finds every relevant notice, however it's worded.

CPV covers everything the public sector buys: supplies (goods), works (construction) and services. When an authority creates a notice, it tags it with one or more CPV codes describing the contract.

## How the numbering works

A CPV code has **up to nine digits**, in the format `XX000000-Y`:

- The **first two digits** identify the **division** (the broad area).
- The **third digit** narrows to a **group**.
- The **fourth** to a **class**.
- The **fifth** to a **category**.
- The **last three digits** (before the dash) add finer precision.
- The **ninth digit**, after the dash, is a **verification (check) digit**.

The deeper you go, the more specific the code. For example, a division covers a whole field like IT or construction; add more digits and you narrow to a particular product or service within it. As a rule, the more zeros at the end, the broader the code.

## Where CPV codes come from

CPV was established by **Regulation (EC) No 2195/2002** and updated by **Regulation (EC) No 213/2008**, which set the version still in use. Although it originated in EU procurement, the UK has retained CPV under the Procurement Act 2023 — notices created on the central digital platform still ask for CPV codes. So the system remains the common reference for classifying UK public contracts.

## How to use CPV codes to find work

CPV codes are a filtering shortcut:

- **Identify the codes that describe what you sell.** Most suppliers map to a handful of codes across one or two divisions.
- **Search by those codes** rather than by keyword alone. Keywords miss notices that use different wording; the code doesn't.
- **Use both broad and specific codes.** A broad division-level code casts a wide net; specific codes cut the noise.

One warning from experience: CPV tagging is inconsistent, and it is worse in some categories than others. Training and workforce development notices in particular get filed under half a dozen plausible codes depending on who wrote the notice. Search by code families *and* keywords, or you will miss relevant work that was simply tagged differently.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does CPV stand for?
Common Procurement Vocabulary — a standard classification system for describing the goods, works and services bought in public procurement.

### How many digits is a CPV code?
Up to nine: an eight-digit code plus a ninth verification (check) digit, written as `XX000000-Y`.

### Are CPV codes still used in the UK?
Yes. The UK retained CPV under the Procurement Act 2023; notices on the central digital platform are still tagged with CPV codes.

### How do I find the right CPV code?
Start from the broad division that matches your field, then narrow down. Browse the official CPV list, and cross-check by looking at the codes used on two or three notices you know are relevant to you.
