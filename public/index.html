How it works

Five steps, from the first conversation to a signed contract. We do the parts you have no reason to be good at.

  1. 1

    We find you

    We go looking for specialist SMEs who already deliver well — often for private clients, or as a subcontractor to someone larger. If you have never bid for public sector work, that is normal. Most of the suppliers we want to work with have not.

  2. 2

    We onboard you

    A structured conversation, not a sales call. What you deliver, where, and at what scale. Who your people are. Which accreditations and insurances you hold. How much delivery capacity you genuinely have next quarter.

    It takes about an hour, and it is the part that makes everything after it work. Vague answers here produce weak bids later.

  3. 3

    We find your angle

    We work out what you can credibly win: which buyers, which contract sizes, which categories. That is usually narrower than suppliers expect, and more winnable than they fear.

    You get an honest read, including the places you are not competitive yet and what would change that.

  4. 4

    Anton watches the market

    Anton is our own bid intelligence engine. It reads every notice published across the UK public sector procurement portals — central government, councils, NHS, housing, police — and scores each one against your specific business, not against a generic keyword list.

    We are not trying to send you fifty tenders a month. Most of what gets published is not winnable for any given supplier, and we would rather say so. What you get is the short list that is actually worth your time.

    A handful you can win, not a firehose
  5. 5

    We bid, and we win it

    We write the submission. All of it — method statements, social value response, quality answers, pricing narrative, compliance paperwork. You give us the technical detail and the evidence; we turn it into a response that scores.

    If we win, you deliver the work and we hold the contract, the reporting, and the client relationship. If we lose, you owe us nothing.

The thin prime model

We win and manage the contract. Specialist SMEs deliver it. The buyer gets one accountable supplier and the expertise of people who do this every day.

1

One accountable prime

A single contract, a single point of contact, and a single organisation carrying the risk. No managing a crowd of small suppliers.

2

Specialists doing the work

Delivery goes to firms who do that one thing well, rather than to whichever team a large prime happens to have on the bench.

3

Proper programme governance

Status reporting, risk and issue management, change control, and financial governance — run by someone whose actual background is programme management.

4

Lower cost to the buyer

A two-person operating model carries almost no overhead, so more of the contract value reaches the people delivering it.

5

Real SME participation

Every contract we win routes public spending to small and medium suppliers who could not have bid for it alone.

6

Decisions in hours

Two people, no approval chain. When a clarification deadline is on Friday, we answer on Wednesday.

For suppliers

We are not asking you to work for us. We are offering access to contracts you cannot realistically win on your own.

What you get

  • A bid pipeline you didn't have to build. We identify and qualify the opportunities. You are not reading tender portals at half past ten at night.
  • The bid written for you. Not reviewed, not coached — written. We need your technical input and evidence, not your evenings.
  • No cost unless you win. No retainer and no subscription, so trying this costs you time and nothing else.
  • The prime's paperwork handled. Contract management, reporting and client relationship sit with us, so you stay on delivery.
  • Public sector references. Two or three delivered contracts change what you can bid for on your own afterwards.
  • Non-exclusive. Exclusivity applies per bid, not to your business. Keep bidding elsewhere.

What we look for

  • A real trading company. Active UK company or established sole trader with a clean filing history.
  • Evidence you have done this before. At least two verifiable case studies or references. Public sector is a bonus, not a requirement.
  • Insurance in place. Typically £2M public liability and £1M professional indemnity — the levels most public sector buyers ask for.
  • Relevant accreditation. Where your category has one — an approved centre status, an awarding body registration — held and current.
  • Honest capacity. A straight answer on how many delivery days you actually have. Overcommitting is the fastest way to lose a contract.
  • Comfort with subcontracting. You deliver, we hold the contract. That arrangement needs to suit you.
