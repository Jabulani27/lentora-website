What we write

All of it. A competitive submission for a £100k contract runs to several thousand words against a published scoring matrix — that is the part we take off you.

We do

  • Method statements — how the work will actually be delivered, mapped to what the buyer is scoring.
  • Quality and technical answers — the bulk of the marks on most public sector tenders.
  • Social value response — specific, measurable commitments rather than good intentions. Often where SMEs under-sell themselves.
  • Selection questionnaire / PQQ — including the insurance, policy and financial evidence the buyer asks for.
  • Pricing narrative — the justification around your numbers, not the numbers themselves.
  • Compliance and submission — portal mechanics, clarifications, deadlines, formatting rules.

You do

  • Technical substance. How you would deliver it, what your method is, where the risks are. Usually a couple of hours on a call.
  • Evidence. Case studies, references, CVs, certificates, accreditations. Once assembled, it is reusable on every future bid.
  • Your price. You know your costs; we would be guessing.
  • An honest capacity answer. If you cannot deliver it, say so before we bid, not after we win.
  • The delivery. The part you are actually good at.

Common questions

How much do bid writing services cost?

Most bid writing consultancies charge a day rate or a fixed fee per submission — commonly £1,500 to £6,000 for a mid-sized tender, payable whether you win or lose. Lentora charges nothing up front. Our fee is 5–10% of contract value and only applies to contracts we win.

What is included in the bid writing?

The whole submission: method statements, quality and technical answers, social value response, pricing narrative, the selection questionnaire or PQQ, and all compliance paperwork. You provide technical detail and evidence; we do the writing and the submission.

What is the difference between a bid writer and a thin prime contractor?

A bid writer writes a submission for you and you hold the resulting contract. A thin prime writes the submission and holds the contract itself, subcontracting delivery to you. That is why there is no fee unless we win — we carry the bid cost and the contractual risk, and take a share of contract value instead.

Do you work with small businesses with no public sector track record?

Yes. Most suppliers we work with have never held a public sector contract. We need evidence you deliver well — typically two verifiable case studies or references, appropriate insurance, and an honest answer on capacity — but public sector experience is not a requirement. Our guide for small businesses covers what the barrier actually is.

How long does a tender submission take?

A typical invitation to tender allows three to four weeks. We need your technical input early in that window, usually a couple of hours, and we handle the rest. Deciding to bid in the final week almost never produces a winning submission.

How do you find the tenders in the first place?

Anton, our own bid intelligence engine, reads every notice published across the UK public sector procurement portals and scores each one against your specific business. Our guide to where to find UK tenders explains the portals if you would rather watch them yourself, and our directory of UK public sector buying frameworks covers the organisations that run the frameworks worth aiming at.
